# Integrity signals and calibration

## AI-use signs

Stronger:
- A method, notation, or concept not taught in the course and unusual for the level, used correctly and without working.
- Typed or rendered content, or a photo or screenshot of a screen or chat interface, where handwriting was expected.
- Text or a chat artefact inside the answer: "Here's the solution", "Step 1:" headers with markdown-style bold, "Certainly", "Note that...", LaTeX source.
- Handwriting that transcribes an answer: final-form derivations with no scratch work, crossings-out, or dead ends, especially alongside misunderstanding elsewhere in the same script.
- Hallucination-shaped errors: confident, well-structured working with an invented formula, or a value that does not follow from the preceding line.
- Solving a differently worded version of the question, or answering with assumptions the paper never stated.

Weaker, and only supporting on their own:
- Unusually tidy structure, explicit "Given / Required / Solution" scaffolding (many students are taught this).
- Quality far above or below the student's other questions.
- Metadata: a capture time long before the exam opened or a messaging-app filename (`PHOTO-...`, `WhatsApp`). This shows the file passed through a phone or app, which is also how honest students move photos.
- Very fast completion with high quality.

Not evidence: neat handwriting, correct answers, strong marks, scanner apps, English quality, non-native phrasing.

## Cross-student signs

Stronger:
- Byte-identical or visually near-identical pages between students.
- The same wrong value, wrong sign, or arithmetic slip reached by the same path, where the base rate is low.
- The same uncommon method, unusual variable names, invented labels, or an identical diagram.
- Identical line-by-line layout, including line breaks and the same crossed-out step.

Supporting:
- Close submission or capture times within a cluster with matching content.
- A matching file-naming pattern within a cluster (weak: students share habits).

Not evidence: the same correct answer via the taught method; a common misconception.

## Calibration

| Cheating % | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0-15 | Nothing notable beyond normal variation |
| 16-35 | One weak or ambiguous indicator |
| 36-60 | Several indicators, with a plausible benign account |
| 61-85 | Multiple independent strong indicators |
| 86-100 | Effectively direct proof, such as an identical file or page across students |

| Confidence % | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 30-50 | Short, partly illegible, or too little work to judge |
| 51-70 | Readable, with evidence that is indirect or could be explained away |
| 71-85 | Clear, verifiable evidence with few alternatives |
| 86-100 | Direct, independently checkable evidence |

Without any way to compare a student's in-person work, AI percentages rarely deserve more than 85, and confidence rarely more than 75, unless a screen capture or chat text is visible.
